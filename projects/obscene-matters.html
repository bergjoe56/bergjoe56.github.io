Studio: Obscene Matters

Studio: Obscene Matters was a semester-long course that focused on architectural timelines and adaptation. We started our project by analyzing different media types and converting them into an architectural language. This was expressed through the use of double studs, simulated decay, and material experimentation. We noted the architectural binaries that were present, and focused on ways to either exaggerate or mute them. From here, we diagrammed different ways in which music, literature, design and human experience affect the use of a built environment. This was the foundation of the final model.

The final project started with a large plywood box that we were given complete creative control over. We were also given historical home construction drawings to use how we saw fit. My model included a visual representation of transitions from other media forms, and this slowly led you to a scale model of the historical home, with interior scenes depicting the future of this site. My depiction includes the Woodland Pattern group assuming control and managing the site. The home would include brackets that allow for the wallboard to be removed and preserved. This would open the option for members or visitors to impact the space and write directly on the walls. My model included this aspect, as I attached drywall to my box, and allowed my fellow classmates and students to write on my model, and leave their impact.
